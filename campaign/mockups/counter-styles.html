Counter styles

Five ways to build the + and - controls. All demos work: click, hold a button to repeat, use the arrow keys on a focused value. Gold, XP, class tokens and quest requirements are the real use cases.

1. Segmented stepper

One bordered pill, minus, value, plus. Compact, familiar, good everywhere.

Best for: HUD counters, token counts, quest requirements. Disabled state at the limits.

2. Value with stacked chevrons

Number first, a split up/down column on the right like a spin box. Reads as a number, not as two buttons.

Best for: header values (XP, gold) where the number should dominate and space is tight.

3. Round dial buttons

Large round touch targets and a big accent number. Tactile, for phone and tablet play.

Best for: the phone view and key class counters you press every turn.

4. Inline, controls on hover

Looks like plain text until you hover or focus. The number is editable by typing. Always visible on touch screens.

Best for: dense tables and lists (enhancements, quest requirements) where many counters share a row.

5. Pips and step chips

Small bounded counts as tappable pips, large values with -10 -1 +1 +10 chips.

Best for: perks, masteries and tokens with a small maximum, and gold or XP when you change by tens after a scenario.
